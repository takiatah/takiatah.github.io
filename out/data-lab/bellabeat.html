Data Analysis

Bellabeat Case Study

An analysis of Fitbit activity and sleep data to explore user behavior and potential opportunities for a wellness technology product.

  • Python
  • Pandas
  • SQL
  • Tableau
View Source Code(opens GitHub in a new tab)Kaggle Notebook(opens in a new tab)Tableau Dashboard(opens in a new tab)

Overview

This Google Data Analytics capstone analyzes how consumers use smart fitness devices, using Fitbit activity and sleep records. The work combines Python (Pandas) for cleaning, SQL for exploratory queries, and Tableau for the published dashboards below.

Problem / business question

Bellabeat is a high-tech wellness company that manufactures health-focused smart products for women. The company wants to better understand how consumers use smart fitness devices and use those insights to support future marketing decisions.

The case-study goal is to identify behavioral trends in Fitbit activity and sleep data and recommend how marketing and the Bellabeat app could improve engagement.

Dataset and source

Source: Fitbit Fitness Tracker Data by Möbius on Kaggle (CC0 public domain). Respondents to a distributed Amazon Mechanical Turk survey contributed data between 12 March 2016 and 12 May 2016.

The notebook uses daily activity for two export windows (12 Mar–11 Apr and 12 Apr–12 May), hourly calories, intensities, and steps for the same windows, and daily sleep from the April–May window.

Cleaning and preparation

Daily activity files from the two months were concatenated, then duplicate Id + ActivityDate rows on the overlapping 12 April 2016 date were dropped, keeping the later export. Dates were parsed and a day-of-week column was added.

Hourly calories, intensities, and steps were inner-joined on Id and ActivityHour within each month, stacked, and de-duplicated. Hours were converted to datetime, with a 12-hour clock label for charts. Sleep rows were parsed and de-duplicated on Id + SleepDay.

The cleaned tables were exported to a SQLite database (daily_activity, hourly_activity, sleep_day) for the SQL analysis.

Methodology

SQL aggregations produced per-user averages, day-of-week summaries, and weekday vs. weekend hourly profiles. Users were classified two ways:

  • Exercise minutes: average of FairlyActiveMinutes + 2 × VeryActiveMinutes, compared with a 21.4-minute daily threshold for recommended moderate-equivalent activity.
  • Step volume: average daily steps, using sedentary / low active / somewhat active / active / highly active cutoffs at 5,000, 7,500, 10,000, and 12,500 steps.

Sleep hours were joined to daily activity on user and date. Sedentary minutes were converted to hours and plotted against hours asleep. Tableau dashboards present those query results.

Sleep dashboard

Only Sunday’s average (7.55 hours) sits inside the 7–9 hour band marked on the dashboard. The scatter of sedentary hours versus hours asleep slopes downward: more sitting is associated with less sleep in this sample. That is an observed association, not a causal claim.

Verified findings

  • Most users are not getting the recommended 8,000–10,000 steps and fall within the sedentary to somewhat active categories (28 of 35 users, from the step-classification dashboard).
  • Saturday had the highest average activity levels, with 7,752 steps and an average intensity score of 12.33.
  • Sunday is the day with least activity (6,607 steps and 10.74 intensity) but the most sleep (7.55 hours), indicating that it is a rest day.
  • Users who had more sedentary minutes tended to sleep less, suggesting a potential negative relationship between sleep duration and sedentary behavior.
  • On weekdays, activity is affected by work schedule and most activity is from 5 PM to 7 PM.
  • On weekends, activity is more consistent throughout the day and higher from 12 PM to 2 PM.

Recommendations

  1. The Bellabeat marketing team should focus marketing primarily on everyday women instead of more active athletes, since that is the majority of smart fitness device users. Marketing should emphasize a woman’s well-being and being healthy over being active, and everyday activities like cleaning and going to work should be acknowledged to encourage women.
  2. The Bellabeat app should send motivating messages throughout the day and recommend workouts on Saturday noons and Tuesday evenings, since people are most active around these times. The Bellabeat app should send calming messages on Sunday and recommend relaxing activities like yoga to reduce stress from the week because Sunday seems to be a rest day.
  3. The Bellabeat app should give incentives for getting 8 hours of sleep and getting 8,000 steps a day. Incentives can be a streak count or points that can be used to unlock features in the app, customize the app, or can be applied to a discount for future Bellabeat purchases. The app should also encourage users to take a short walk after long periods of sedentary activity to improve sleep and help them meet the recommended daily step goals.

Limitations

  • The dataset contains data from a relatively small sample of Fitbit users.
  • Data was collected between March and May 2016, so it is outdated.
  • Sleep data was not available for all participants or throughout the whole time period.
  • The dataset represents Fitbit users and may not fully reflect Bellabeat’s target customer base.

Tools used

Python, Pandas, NumPy, SQL (SQLite), Tableau, and Kaggle Jupyter notebooks.
